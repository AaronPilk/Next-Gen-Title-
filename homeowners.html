The basics

What is title insurance?

Title insurance protects both you and your mortgage lender from losses stemming from defects in the property's title. Defects include improperly recorded documents, forgery, easements, and encroachments. Without it, buyers risk substantial financial liability.

Cost

A one-time, up-front fee — not an ongoing expense.

Combined owner and lender policies typically cost 0.5–1.0% of the purchase price.

That's approximately $1,500–$3,000 on a $300,000 home, according to ALTA. You pay once, and your protection lasts for as long as you own the property.

What it protects against

Coverage

  • Property survey errors
  • Boundary disputes
  • Deed errors
  • Building code violations
  • Conflicting wills
  • Ex-spouse claims
  • Forged documents
  • Contractor, tax, and lender liens
  • Encroachments
  • Improperly recorded documents
Good to know

Exclusions

Title insurance doesn't protect homeowners against all possible infringements on their property rights.

It excludes issues arising from your own actions — such as unpaid contractors or tax defaults — as well as eminent domain seizures.
